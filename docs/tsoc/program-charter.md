# TSOC Personal Incident Response Pilot Charter

## Program Purpose
The Teaching Security Operations Center (TSOC) Personal Incident Response Pilot is a training-first program that prepares student analysts to support university individuals experiencing personal cybersecurity incidents.

This pilot uses Backdoors and Breaches gameplay to simulate realistic incident response decisions, communication, and escalation behavior in a controlled environment.

## Mission Statement
Develop student analysts who can provide calm, victim-centered, and technically sound guidance for personal cyber incidents while respecting university governance boundaries.

## Pilot Objectives
- Train students to perform structured personal incident triage.
- Build consistency in escalation and referral decisions.
- Improve incident communication quality during high-stress scenarios.
- Produce measurable outcomes to support a future guided-support service.

## In Scope (Pilot)
- Classroom and lab-based simulation sessions only.
- Student role-play for intake, triage, communication, and incident command.
- Scenario-based referral recommendations to existing university support units.
- After-action reviews and coaching based on documented performance criteria.

## Out of Scope (Pilot)
- No live incident handling for real individuals.
- No endpoint forensics, malware removal, or privileged account actions.
- No direct contact with external law enforcement or legal offices by students.
- No storage of real victim PII in pilot artifacts.

## Service Boundary Statement
This pilot is an educational simulation program and does not replace the university's production Security Operations Center, legal counsel, IT support channels, or emergency services.

## Roles and Accountability
- **Pilot Sponsor:** Supervisor and TSOC leadership.
- **Facilitator:** Oversees simulation execution and scoring consistency.
- **Student Analysts:** Execute assigned roles, document decisions, and complete reflections.
- **Observer/Evaluator:** Tracks rubric outcomes and records actionable feedback.

## Escalation and Referral Rules (Simulation)
When a scenario reaches one of the conditions below, students must execute referral language and document the route:

- **Safety risk or credible threat:** Refer to campus safety pathway.
- **University account/system compromise:** Refer to IT helpdesk and production SOC workflow.
- **Potential legal, compliance, or privacy harm:** Escalate through supervisor to designated legal/privacy contacts.
- **Financial fraud indicators:** Recommend financial institution and identity protection reporting steps.

## Severity Model for Training
- **Sev 1 (Critical):** Active harm, extortion, or major account compromise in progress.
- **Sev 2 (High):** Confirmed account takeover or high-confidence compromise indicators.
- **Sev 3 (Moderate):** Suspicious activity without confirmed compromise.
- **Sev 4 (Low):** Preventive guidance and hygiene support.

## Data Handling Rules for Pilot Artifacts
- Use synthetic or anonymized scenario data only.
- Do not record passwords, MFA secrets, SSNs, or payment card details.
- Keep pilot notes in approved project storage only.
- Retain session notes only for educational measurement and program improvement.

## Success Criteria
- At least 80% of participants make correct escalation choices by final pilot sessions.
- Median triage decision time improves across cohorts.
- Facilitator ratings show improvement in communication clarity and empathy.
- Sponsor review confirms readiness to propose a guided-support phase.
